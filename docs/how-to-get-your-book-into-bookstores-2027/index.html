How to get your book into bookstores in 2027

Short answer

Bookstores order from wholesale catalogs, not from authors. Make your book orderable first: an ISBN under a publisher name, clean metadata and a listing with the wholesalers stores already use. Then make it worth stocking: terms the trade accepts and a reason for a store to believe its customers will buy it.

Start here

How bookstores actually buy books

Bookstores order most of their books from wholesalers. One account covers thousands of publishers, with one invoice and fast delivery.

Catalogs, not inboxes

Booksellers search wholesale catalogs that list millions of titles. They do not browse author websites or answer cold emails.

Terms the whole trade uses

A store buys below the list price, expects a trade discount and often the option to return unsold copies.

Same route for every book

A self-published book reaches a bookstore the same way as any other: listed where booksellers order, on terms they accept.

Before anyone can order

Five things a bookstore needs from your book

Without the first three, the book cannot be ordered at all. Without the last two, it can be ordered but is unlikely to be stocked.

  • An ISBN and a publisher name – it identifies the edition and puts it into Books in Print
  • Complete metadata – title, author, format, price, date, subject categories
  • Wholesale availability – through the wholesaler the store already buys from
  • A trade discount – the store lives on the difference to the list price
  • Returnability – many booksellers only buy books they can send back

The key distinction

Orderable is not the same as stocked

Print on demand makes a book orderable wherever the wholesaler reaches. A store only puts copies on its shelf if it expects them to sell.

Every copy on a shelf is money the store has already spent.

Why it rarely happens

Why most self-published books never reach the shelf

No sales machinery

Traditional publishers bring sales reps, seasonal catalogs, advance copies and budgets. A self-published title usually arrives with none of these.

Terms that shift the risk

A book that is non-returnable or carries a short discount can be orderable, but the store takes all the risk on every copy.

No known publisher

A publisher the store knows, listed with its usual wholesaler, makes the yes much easier.

Special orders still count

A customer asks, the store orders one copy, the customer picks it up: a real sale through a real bookstore.

Part 1

Make your book orderable

These three steps decide whether a bookseller can order your book at all.

  1. 01

    Get an ISBN under a publisher name

    The ISBN and the imprint travel with every record a bookseller sees. See what a publishing imprint is.

  2. 02

    File complete metadata

    Title, author, format, price, publication date and subject categories, in the directories of every market you sell in.

  3. 03

    Get listed with the wholesalers

    Stores order on the account they already have. If your book is not in that catalog, the usual way to order it does not exist.

Part 2

Make your book worth stocking

Being listed opens the door. These steps give a bookseller a reason to walk through it.

  1. 04

The most asked-for chain

How to get your book into Barnes & Noble

What B&N's own help pages say, as of October 2026.

Listing comes from data

B&N lists titles from bibliographic data the publisher supplies: ISBN, title, author, publisher, format, price, date, BISAC and discount.

Send it early

The record goes to B&N ideally at least 180 days before publication.

No paid placement

B&N states it will never offer in-store placement for your books in exchange for money.

The route in

Wholesale distribution under a publisher, listed in the catalogs B&N stores already order from.

Independent bookstores

Consignment: what to get in writing first

The store takes a few copies without paying upfront and pays you for the ones that sell. Terms differ from store to store.

  • The split between you and the store
  • Any intake or handling fee
  • How long the books stay on display
  • How and when you are paid
  • What happens to damaged or unsold copies
  • Works best with a local subject, an event or a reading group

In person

How to pitch your book to a local bookstore

  1. 01

    Go at a quiet time

    Ask who handles ordering. Bring a copy of the book.

  2. 02

    Bring a one-page sheet

    ISBN, price, format, publisher, a short description and how the store can order it.

  3. 03

    Be a customer first

    Booksellers notice who supports their store.

  4. 04

    Know your terms

    Discount, returnability and wholesaler are the first questions a buyer asks.

  5. 05

    Offer something

    A reading, a signing or a talk gives the store a reason to stock and promote copies.

  6. 06

    Accept a no

    Shelf space is limited, and a store's decision about its own shelves is final.

Why bookstores are different

Catalogs, not star ratings

On Amazon

A self-published book is found through its sales, and its reviews decide whether readers buy. With few reviews, one one-star rating pulls the average down.

In the book trade

A bookseller looks at the publisher, the ISBN, the price and the terms. No reviews or ad budget are needed for a store to order your book.

Questions

Getting into bookstores, answered

Do bookstores buy books directly from authors?

Usually not. Most stores order through wholesalers so they can buy many publishers' books on one account. Some independent stores take local authors' books on consignment.

Can a print-on-demand book be sold in bookstores?

Yes. Listed with a wholesaler, it can be ordered by any store that uses that wholesaler. Whether a store keeps copies on the shelf depends on the terms and expected demand.

Will bookstores put my book on their shelves?

Every store that orders from these catalogs can order it, for a customer or for its own shelf. What a store keeps on the shelf is the bookseller's choice.

How long until my book appears in stores?

With Shelf Publisher, usually about 8 to 9 days after you submit it, in most cases within two weeks; some retailers take longer.
